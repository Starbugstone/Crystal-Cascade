The Prospect Hollow demo adds a small story to ordinary Crystal Cascade play. Six fixed plots can be repaired, Juniper house has one additional upgrade, and households arrive automatically when the well, farm, and home are ready. There are no production rates, worker assignments, construction timers, or offline penalties.

**Try the demo.** Run `npm ci` and `npm run dev`. The game opens on the town. Choose Go mining, finish a puzzle, and select Visit town on the results screen. Repair the well, farm, and house to welcome the Bell family. Continue playing or replay unlocked levels to restore the saloon, stables, and sheriff's office, and improve the house for two more neighbors. The Mine tab retains the campaign collection.

The optional Stories from the trail section appears after the first family arrives. Read the warning, then continue to the encounter. A built sheriff stops the bandits. Otherwise they take at most 10 coins or 10% of the balance, while leaving at least 50 coins if the player has that much. Below that reserve, the encounter is harmless. It resolves once per profile, including across reloads. It never occurs in the background. Unit fixtures exercise both outcomes; manually testing the alternative should use a separate browser profile, preserving the original save.

**Economy and story tuning.** `src/data/town.js` contains the building catalog, costs, prerequisites, artwork positions, and character dialogue. `src/game/town/TownRules.js` owns pure payout, purchase, population, goal, normalization, and bandit rules. A completed run earns `50 + min(50, floor(ordinaryJewelsRemoved / 2))` coins. Each introductory repair costs 50; the saloon, stables, and sheriff cost 100 each; the home's additional wing costs 150. Completing everything costs 600 coins, reachable in 12 minimum-paying runs with no bandit loss. Typical jewel payouts shorten this; tune the 10–20 minute target from player feedback rather than assuming it from an automated walkthrough.

The first family requires all three basic services and adds two residents. The larger home adds two more. Other visible workers and visitors are ambient characters outside the household count. Upgrades, population, and events are action-driven; animation and elapsed wall-clock time never award anything. The current story progresses only through the Old West chapter. Modern facades, water towers, garages and early cars, recovery adventures, and later chapters remain ideas in [issue #9](https://github.com/Starbugstone/Crystal-Cascade/issues/9).

**Saving and the future backend.** Campaign records, best times, powers, town state, and run sequence markers save as one versioned snapshot under `crystal-cascade-profile-v2`. A legacy `crystal-cascade-campaign-v1` save is loaded if no new profile exists; the original key is retained as a migration backup. The first successful save writes the new profile. Coin settlement is coupled to campaign/chest settlement, before any result animation. Purchases validate the expected stage; abandoned/replaced runs and repeated completion calls cannot award money again.

`campaignStore` remains the single owner of progress, including power spending. `src/services/localProfile.js` isolates browser storage. Town rendering calls store actions rather than editing the wallet. The [Symfony backend in issue #10](https://github.com/Starbugstone/Crystal-Cascade/issues/10) can replace this local persistence/action boundary with authoritative API actions after the gameplay is evaluated.

This demo is local and intended for one active tab. Refreshes and ordinary restarts keep progress; clearing browser data or losing the device can lose it. Local data is editable and provides no anti-cheat guarantee. No network API or account is required. Storage failures show a warning and keep gameplay available in memory. Unreadable/future-version profiles are preserved without overwriting them, with an explanatory warning. Export a copy of the stored JSON before any manual reset or recovery experiment.

**Art and accessibility.** `TownBuilding.vue` and `TownMap.vue` contain original, editable SVG artwork created for this demo. The small, fixed town uses SVG/CSS instead of another Phaser instance: it scales across screens, does not download raster textures, and adds no second JavaScript render loop. Ruins and repairs share footprints; fresh roofs, water, planted fields, garden boxes, saloon awnings, horses, a wagon, and a new house wing make progress visible. Existing gem assets remain unchanged.

There are six selectable map plots plus an equivalent HTML building list. Both support keyboard selection. The building panel explains prerequisites and missing funds, and changes announce through a status region. Mobile has an always-available mining action. Reduced motion disables resident loops and repair reveals; high contrast strengthens text and outlines. Resident animation pauses when the page is hidden or settings are open. Leaving town unmounts its scene and clears its listeners/reveal timer. Town audio is intentionally quiet in this first pass; the existing puzzle audio and settings are retained.

**Verification and playtest notes.** `testing/town.test.js` covers the minimum-income path through every upgrade, requirement failures, duplicate/unknown purchases, households, capped bandit loss/protection, legacy migration, profile recovery, power spending alongside purchases, stale/completed runs, real jewel removal metadata, overlapping clears, chained/frozen/obstacle exclusions, and refill jewels across cascades. Existing campaign, expansion, inventory, victory, active-time, engine, and animation tests remain in the suite.

The browser walkthrough completed the first three real puzzles using mouse and emulated touch gestures: 46, 49, and 57 ordinary jewels earned 73, 74, and 78 coins. Repairing the well, farm, and home left 75 coins and welcomed the first two neighbors. The optional unprotected encounter took 7 coins, leaving 68; the buildings, campaign, inventory, and event survived reload. A separate later-state fixture supplied coins to exercise the remaining purchases, the larger home, and the sheriff's zero-loss outcome through the UI.

The walkthrough checked desktop (1440 × 1050), portrait (390 × 844 and 320 × 568), and landscape (844 × 390), keyboard building selection, reduced motion, high contrast, and Town/Mine navigation. The next repair and coin cost were clear during the introduction; on phones, the building panel sits below the map, so selection scrolls it into view and a fixed mining action keeps the next puzzle accessible. The SVG repairs and new residents give each purchase a visible result. These are implementation walkthrough observations, not audience research.
