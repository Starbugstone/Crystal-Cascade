# Prospect Hollow demo

Crystal Cascade opens on an English/French landing page explaining the jewel puzzles and the village. Enter the village, choose a construction, then click the mine entrance or its label to play the next unfinished level. Results return to the village. Welcome, Village, and Mine remain available in the header on desktop and phones; the Museum tab appears once it is built. Mine starts the next unfinished puzzle and stays selected while playing; clicking it during a run keeps that run. After the campaign is complete it opens the museum, where completed levels live. There is no separate level-selection page.

**The village is the home screen.** Tapping a building or empty plot opens a focused dialog (a bottom sheet on phones). One button starts work and returns to the map. “All plots” opens a directory for off-screen and locked plots, with a shortcut to the building needed to unlock them. Food, water, population and supplies have compact, tappable controls. Neighbor stories and raid replay live in a collapsible journal. The permanent sidebar, long building list, and sticky bottom mining bar have been removed. Museum, construction and Settings dialogs pause the 3D activity; native dialogs provide focus containment, Escape/backdrop dismissal, and focus restoration.

**A small village, with automatic basic needs.** The original eight plots start bare. Choose any original plot for free first materials, and fund multiple constructions when you can afford them. Every normal puzzle completion advances all active projects once. Builder hammers advance one chosen project. Buildings finish at **level 3**, including the initial build; their existing services remain active during improvements. Wooden scaffolding, platforms, diagonal braces, a ladder, and material stacks surround the existing 3D building during upgrades. New benefits activate only when work finishes.

Upgrading the original **home to level 2** unlocks three extra residential plots; the original **well to level 2** unlocks one extra well; the original **farm to level 2** unlocks two extra farms. There are **14 plots in total**, all with three completed levels. Locked plots remain prairie in 3D and have their requirement in the building list. Extra plots use the same cost, duration, and service progression as their original type. Population is the minimum of completed housing capacity, water capacity, and food capacity; the town can welcome **24 residents** with all four homes at level 3 and sufficient farms/wells. There is no food depletion or manual supply chore.

The catalog is in `src/data/town.js`. Each funded project stores its own `required` count so later tuning does not change its promised duration; old three/four-puzzle projects retain their work. The expanded catalog contains 180 individual construction steps, shared across concurrent projects when they are funded together. Costs and durations rise across completed levels; normal mining payouts remain generous for this demo.

**A lived-in village.** Completed building levels add up to a development score. Dirt paths appear at 3 points, wooden boardwalks at 12, and more defined roads with street lamps at 24. Residents walk and work, customers enter the saloon, stay inside briefly, and leave again; stables add articulated mounted travelers following the streets. Horses have jointed legs, moving heads and tails, reins, saddles, and seated riders. Matching actor parts use GPU instancing to reduce draw calls as the cast grows. The continuous prairie, orbit/zoom controls, camera clearance, branched trees, and warm palette remain.

**Saloon income.** With a completed saloon and residents, automatically earn **6 × completed saloon level × completed house count coins per hour** (6–72/hour). Completed houses count once each; their upgrades increase housing capacity rather than multiplying saloon income. Save up to **8 hours of earnings while away**, then bank it on return. Earnings also settle every 30 seconds while visible, on visibility changes, and before building changes. Settle at the old rate before an improvement finishes. Integer coin-millisecond remainders preserve fractional earnings; retries at the same time and clock rollback cannot duplicate time already credited. Older saves start their income clock on first load. The saloon sheet shows its live hourly rate and last automatic credit, with a compact hourly-rate shortcut above the map. This browser clock is a demo mechanism; authoritative time belongs in the Symfony follow-up.

**Mounted bandit raids.** The first encounter can happen when residents move in. Further encounters can happen on returning to the village after five more normal puzzle completions; continuous play does not advance the raid counter. There are no timed/offline attacks. Gangs have **2 riders below 16 development points, 4 from 16, and 6 from 30**. Each completed sheriff level protects against two riders; upgrades retain the previous level’s protection until finished.

The outcome and wallet deduction are saved **before** the animation: riders arrive from the ridge, fire stylized warning shots, approach the mine and a completed building, and leave with sacks if they stole coins. Sheriff patrols ride out to scare them away. If protection is insufficient, the protected share retreats early while the remaining bandits finish the robbery. Loss is the minimum of `5 × unprotected riders`, 10% of the wallet rounded down, and the amount above a **50-coin reserve**; thus never more than 30 coins. Buildings, inventory and construction progress are never damaged. Save the gang, sheriff level, targets, run counter, receipt ID, outcome, loss, and presentation status together. Reloading an unfinished presentation resumes the saved story without another deduction. “Watch the last raid again” and “Skip animation” never change the outcome. The original one-off event migrates as already seen.

Raids have an approximately 21-second active timeline, pause with Settings, the museum, or a hidden tab, and clear their actors afterward. Reduced motion and unavailable WebGL show the saved outcome immediately with a Continue button. The expanded SVG fallback remains selectable and depicts upgrades/scaffolding.

**The museum.** Build and select the museum, then open its collection inside the village. Normal replay cards show completed levels, stars, best score, and best active time, grouped by chapter. Normal replays retain completion income, chests, and construction progress. The completed museum is required at the game action boundary, including immediate replay from results. Until then the mine always opens the next unfinished puzzle. After the last campaign level, the mine opens the museum (or selects its plot if it still needs building).

Continuous play offers every unlocked level, including the next unfinished one. It keeps the board playable after obstacles and relic objectives are cleared, without a victory interruption. Scores are stored separately from normal campaign records; it grants no stars, chests, level unlocks, builder hammers, or construction steps. Earn one coin per ten collected jewels, capped at **25 coins per level across all continuous visits**. Reopening the mode or reloading cannot refresh that allowance. Once capped, play and personal-best scores continue without more currency. Earnings and personal bests save after scoring resolutions; the board itself is not resumed after reload. Normal puzzle bonuses use the real inventory. Return to the village/museum whenever desired. This lifetime cap is deliberately simple for the demo, not a daily system.

**Chests and supplies.** One server-replaceable weighted roll determines each score/speed chest before its roulette is shown: 70% puzzle bonus, 20% 25 coins, 10% one builder hammer. Within the puzzle-bonus portion, Clear Row and Shuffle retain their 35% weights, and Hammer, Color Wand, and Tile Breaker retain 10% each. Tier labels/eligibility still follow score and speed; there is one prize per chest. Revealing, skipping, or reopening the results never awards again. The roulette displays the actual saved reward and amount, including overflow conversions.

The initial limit is **3 of each puzzle bonus**, raised to 5, 8, then 12 by completed armory tiers. A full slot converts an incoming bonus into 10 coins. Inventory grants and chest settlement share the same bounded path. The armory panel lists each quantity/limit, and the puzzle toolbar shows the limit. An armory finished by a normal victory provides its new capacity to that victory’s chests.

**Builder hammers are distinct from the puzzle Hammer.** Up to five are stored separately, independent of armory tiers. On an active construction, Use a builder hammer consumes exactly one and advances only that plot by one step; its final step activates the building’s benefit immediately. It never advances other plots, awards mining income, or changes campaign records. Stale commands, missing projects, and empty stock cannot spend. Excess hammer rewards also convert into 10 coins each. There are no free starting builder hammers; testing fixtures may seed earned stock.

**Economy tuning.** Normal completion income remains `50 + min(50, floor(collectedJewels / 2))`. Prices and payouts are intentionally generous while testing the loop. Chest coins and overflow are additional income shown in their reward receipts. Later tune a gradual curve of earnings, costs, and required work against beta feedback, preserving funded projects. Continuous mode has its separate small allowance so it cannot indefinitely fund the village. Keep decisions to one clear action, without maintenance bills, resource ratios, workers, or real-time construction timers.

**3D presentation.** `TownDiorama.js` uses original Three.js geometry for timber buildings, staged construction, animated articulated residents, horses, and a mine cart. The museum has a porch and crystal displays; armory upgrades add supply crates and storage. A separate continuous rolling prairie has winding tracks, branched trees, hills, sparse grass, rocks, cacti, and fog. The landscape is built once per scene. Starting construction rebuilds town objects, preserving the camera. Static geometry uses indexed vertex-color batches, while animated parts share instanced geometry and clothing colors. The static view is cached with its depth buffer, so people remain correctly occluded by buildings without redrawing the entire prairie every frame. Camera/building/selection changes and resizing refresh the cache. Pointer camera changes are coalesced per frame; shadows update only when needed. Ambient animation is capped at 30 fps and pixel ratio at 1.5. Windmill rotors and the mine cart share the animated pass. Reduced motion stops ambient animation while manual camera controls remain usable.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, or use the accessible camera buttons. With the canvas focused, arrows rotate, plus/minus zoom, and Home resets the view. The camera remains above terrain. Labels follow the view and hide when they overlap or leave the frame; every plot also remains in the building list. Dragging and pinching do not select plots or enter the mine. Museum dialogs and Settings pause the town. Renderers stay outside Vue’s reactive graph and dispose controls, geometry, materials, textures, observers, and WebGL contexts on exit. The SVG fallback supports the same construction, museum, armory, and mining actions when WebGL is unavailable. The landing illustration reuses original SVG building art and loads no 3D renderer.

**Persistence and accounting.** `localProfile` is the replaceable storage boundary; there is no backend or account requirement. The profile stays under `crystal-cascade-profile-v2` with normal/continuous records, wallet, bounded powers, builder hammers, building stages, projects, events, and run markers. Earlier single-project and campaign-only saves migrate with their recovery key intact. Saved over-cap inventory is converted once into 10 coins per excess item, with a visible explanation; saving/reloading retains the normalized quantities and coin balance. Normal victory settles the campaign, construction, new capacity, chest awards, and mining payout together before presenting results. Continuous receipts validate the active run and track credited earnings independently. Duplicate or stale settlement cannot pay twice.

**Language and tester reset.** All landing, town, museum, armory, puzzle, chest, Settings, sign, and accessibility text follows the first supported browser preference (French or English, otherwise English). A future account setting can use the same locale boundary. Settings → Reset progress for testing asks for confirmation and clears normal/continuous records, allowance usage, powers, hammers, money, buildings, work, raid receipts/counters, and income checkpoints. Cancellation preserves everything. The confirmed reset returns to the bare village and persists across reload.

**Verification.** The test suite covers three-level catalogs, unlocked plot prerequisites, population capacity, raid strength/cadence/receipts, articulated raid lifecycles, actor instancing/visibility, passive income fractions, clock rollback, offline caps, final-step rate settlement, and varied/concurrent construction, legacy durations, last-step benefits, hammer spending and stale requests, every bonus capacity, overflow conversion, reward weighting, atomic coin/hammer settlement, museum eligibility, continuous play past objectives, lifetime allowance across retries/reloads, separate scores, and reset. Existing engine, fusion, input, active-time, reward, camera-clearance, and localization regressions remain in place. The parallel main branch’s expanded fusion behavior and French feedback are preserved.

All **265 tests across 22 files** pass, along with formatting and the production build. `npm run verify` runs all three checks, and Vercel uses it before producing a deployable build. The parallel main branch remains included. Existing Vite size advisories concern deferred Three.js and Phaser chunks; the landing screen loads neither engine.

Final production browser checks cover English desktop (1440 × 1050), French phones (390 × 844 and 320 × 568), and landscape (844 × 390). They exercise the plot bottom sheet, locked-plot shortcut, concurrent improvements, five one-plot hammer steps to level 3, maximum-level actions, both museum modes and active tabs, Escape/close behavior, reset/cancel, and repeated navigation with one canvas. Visiting the museum or tapping Village again retains the town renderer. Continuous-mode controls fit small portrait and landscape screens; its compact banner leaves the board and bonus toolbar visible.

A final mouse-played normal level finished in 10 moves (4,800 points), saved the well at 1/2 steps and offered level 2 at the mine. A separate continuous visit cleared the objectives at move 17 and continued for five further real moves, saving 21,000 points and 14 coins without advancing the well or changing the normal record. Earlier deterministic reward fixtures verified coin/hammer roulettes, overflow and repeat/reload accounting. Later-game construction and raid scenarios use seeded local profiles, without adding test-only controls to the product.

Animated desktop scenarios passed for four bandits against a level 1 sheriff (10 coins taken) and six bandits against level 3 protection (no loss). Settings and museum pauses, actual 3D model picking, orbit/zoom refresh, saved-outcome reload/replay, reduced-motion presentation, the 576-coin maximum offline credit and same-time retry, expanded SVG keyboard access, and complete reset also passed. The only deliberately induced graphics errors were from disabling WebGL for the fallback test.

**Performance measurement:** on Chromium's SwiftShader software renderer at 390 × 844, with all 14 plots at level 3, batching and cached scenery increased steady animation from roughly 4 to 27 frames/second in five-second samples. The final sample had six tasks over 50 ms (maximum 60 ms), compared with repeated 240–320 ms stalls before caching; measured plot-popup response was about 40 ms. This is a repeatable software-rendered stress fixture, not a physical-phone benchmark or a guarantee across devices. Reduced motion and dialog/hidden-tab pauses avoid continuous rendering. Physical-device performance and beta feedback remain launch follow-ups.

Progress belongs to this browser; clearing its data can lose the save. Local state is editable and is not an anti-cheat boundary. The separate Symfony work in issue #10 will provide recoverable accounts and authoritative action/reward accounting.
