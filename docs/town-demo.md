# Prospect Hollow demo

Crystal Cascade opens on an English/French landing page explaining the jewel puzzles and the village. Enter the village, choose a construction, then click the mine entrance or its label to play the next unfinished level. Results offer matching full-width buttons: Continue mining with a pickaxe for the next unfinished level, and Back to village with a house. Welcome, Village, and Mine remain available in the header on desktop and phones; the Museum tab appears once it is built. Mine starts the next unfinished puzzle and stays selected while playing; clicking it during a run keeps that run. After the campaign is complete it opens the museum, where completed levels live. There is no separate level-selection page.

**The village is the home screen.** Tapping a building or empty plot opens a focused dialog (a bottom sheet on phones). One button starts work and returns to the map. “All plots” opens a directory for off-screen and locked plots, with a shortcut to the building needed to unlock them. Food, water, population and supplies have compact, tappable controls. Neighbor stories and raid replay live in a collapsible journal. The permanent sidebar, long building list, and sticky bottom mining bar have been removed. Museum, construction and Settings dialogs pause the 3D activity; native dialogs provide focus containment, Escape/backdrop dismissal, and focus restoration.

**A small village, with automatic basic needs.** The original ten plots start bare. Choose any original plot for free first materials, and fund multiple constructions when you can afford them. Every normal puzzle completion advances all active projects once. Builder hammers advance one chosen project. Buildings finish at **level 3**, including the initial build; their existing services remain active during improvements. Wooden scaffolding, platforms, diagonal braces, a ladder, and material stacks surround the existing 3D building during upgrades. New benefits activate only when work finishes.

Upgrading the original **home to level 2** unlocks three extra residential plots; the original **well to level 2** unlocks one extra well; the original **farm to level 2** unlocks two extra farms. There are **16 plots in total**, all with three completed levels. Locked plots remain prairie in 3D and have their requirement in the building list. Extra plots use the same cost, duration, and service progression as their original type. Population is the minimum of completed housing capacity, water capacity, and food capacity; the town can welcome **24 residents** with all four homes at level 3 and sufficient farms/wells. There is no food depletion or manual supply chore.

The catalog is in `src/data/town.js`. Basic wells, farms, and houses open immediately; all improvements and larger buildings finish after one normal puzzle. Valid old funded basic projects complete on migration; larger old projects are reduced to one remaining puzzle. The first chosen building is still free. Services remain active while upgrading.

**A lived-in village.** Completed building levels add up to a development score. Narrow dirt tracks connect the bare plots from the start; wooden boardwalks arrive at 12 points and street lamps at 24. Lots are spaced seven to eight world units apart, with open yards and a broad ring of prairie before the surrounding hills. Buildings, entrance paths, residents, mounted visitors and raids share the expanded layout. Residents walk and work, customers enter the saloon, stay inside briefly, and leave again; stables add articulated mounted travelers following the streets. Horses have jointed legs, moving heads and tails, reins, saddles, and seated riders. Matching actor parts use GPU instancing to reduce draw calls as the cast grows. The continuous prairie, orbit/zoom controls, camera clearance, branched trees, and warm palette remain.

**Saloon income.** With a completed saloon and residents, automatically earn **6 × completed saloon level × completed house count coins per hour** (6–72/hour). Completed houses count once each; their upgrades increase housing capacity rather than multiplying saloon income. Save up to **8 hours of earnings while away**, then bank it on return. Earnings also settle every 30 seconds while visible, on visibility changes, and before building changes. Settle at the old rate before an improvement finishes. Integer coin-millisecond remainders preserve fractional earnings; retries at the same time and clock rollback cannot duplicate time already credited. Older saves start their income clock on first load. The saloon sheet shows its live hourly rate and last automatic credit, with a compact hourly-rate shortcut above the map. This browser clock is a demo mechanism; authoritative time belongs in the Symfony follow-up.

**Mounted bandit raids.** The first encounter can happen when residents move in. Further encounters can happen on returning to the village after five more normal puzzle completions; continuous play does not advance the raid counter. There are no timed/offline attacks. Gangs have **2 riders below 16 development points, 4 from 16, and 6 from 30**. For each service, bank or sheriff, a completed level covers half the risk from two riders, capped at half the gang’s total risk. Both need the appropriate level for full protection; upgrades retain existing protection until finished.

The outcome and wallet deduction are saved **before** the animation: riders arrive from the ridge, fire stylized warning shots, approach the mine and a completed building, and leave with sacks if they stole coins. Sheriff patrols ride out to scare them away. If protection is insufficient, the protected share retreats early while the remaining bandits finish the robbery. Loss is the minimum of `ceil(5 × riders × (1 - combinedProtection))`, 10% of the wallet rounded down, and the amount above a **50-coin reserve**; thus never more than 30 coins. Buildings, inventory and construction progress are never damaged. Save the gang, sheriff and bank levels, targets, run counter, receipt ID, outcome, loss, and presentation status together. Reloading an unfinished presentation resumes the saved story without another deduction. “Watch the last raid again” and “Skip animation” never change the outcome. The original one-off event migrates as already seen.

Raids have an approximately 21-second active timeline, pause with Settings, the museum, or a hidden tab, and clear their actors afterward. Reduced motion and unavailable WebGL show the saved outcome immediately with a Continue button. The expanded SVG fallback remains selectable and depicts upgrades/scaffolding.

**The museum.** Build and select the museum, then open its collection inside the village. Normal replay cards show completed levels, stars, best score, and best active time, grouped by chapter. Normal replays retain completion income, chests, and construction progress. The completed museum is required at the game action boundary, including immediate replay from results. Until then the mine always opens the next unfinished puzzle. After the last campaign level, the mine opens the museum (or selects its plot if it still needs building).

Continuous play offers every unlocked level, including the next unfinished one. It keeps the board playable after obstacles and relic objectives are cleared, without a victory interruption. Scores are stored separately from normal campaign records; it grants no stars, chests, level unlocks, builder hammers, or construction steps. Earn one coin per ten collected jewels, capped at **25 coins per level across all continuous visits**. Reopening the mode or reloading cannot refresh that allowance. Once capped, play and personal-best scores continue without more currency. Earnings and personal bests save after scoring resolutions; the board itself is not resumed after reload. Normal puzzle bonuses use the real inventory. Return to the village/museum whenever desired. This lifetime cap is deliberately simple for the demo, not a daily system.

**Chests and roulette.** Each earned score/speed chest contains one reward. Automatic rolls remain 70% puzzle powers, 20% coins and 10% builder hammers; a persisted counter guarantees a hammer within ten rolls. Completion saves pending receipts and fallback awards. Tap the moving roulette itself to claim the symbol nearest its selection line. The sharp, steady reel gives each item a 350 ms selection window and shows the full selection twice before stopping automatically after 4.9 seconds. Tapping the prize continues. Skip, exit, next-run and reload paths claim unopened fallback rewards once. Each choice removes its receipt in the same local save as the inventory update. A cheerful original chime replaces the bomb explosion.

Inventory starts at **zero**, with an initial limit of **3 of each puzzle bonus**, raised to 5, 8, then 12 by completed armory tiers. A full slot converts an incoming bonus into 10 coins. Inventory grants and chest settlement share the same bounded path. The armory panel lists each quantity/limit, and the puzzle toolbar shows the limit. An armory finished by a normal victory provides its new capacity to that victory’s chests.

**Builder hammers are distinct from the puzzle Hammer.** Up to five are stored separately, independent of armory tiers. On an active construction, Use a builder hammer consumes exactly one and advances only that plot by one step; its final step activates the building’s benefit immediately. It never advances other plots, awards mining income, or changes campaign records. Stale commands, missing projects, and empty stock cannot spend. Excess hammer rewards also convert into 10 coins each. There are no free starting builder hammers; testing fixtures may seed earned stock.

**Shop.** Each completed normal mine run refreshes two, three or four distinct offers at shop levels 1–3. Each offer is one item; upgrades add a shelf without restocking sold offers. Clear Row/Shuffle cost 40 coins, other puzzle powers 60, builder hammers 75. Buying validates the completed shop, current stock generation, unsold status, funds, and armory/hammer capacity before debiting. Stock and purchases persist; abandoning, reloading and continuous play never reroll the shop.

**Tour, fullscreen, and mine appearance.** New villages show a six-part illustrated introduction, with a dismiss action and a route to the first building; the tour can be reopened from the village info icon. Food/water/population controls open the relevant building. Fullscreen expands the map to the mobile viewport with an exit icon, Escape, scroll restoration and resize handling. Completed chapter boundaries add ten cumulative cosmetic mine upgrades at no cost, in both 3D and SVG. The mining (i) guide pauses the clock and introduces each obstacle once; its illustrations match the new seal/chain/exit art and hit badges.

**Audio.** `TownSoundscape` synthesizes original low-volume music and life sounds. Birds and mining are always eligible; chatter needs residents, hammering needs an active construction, and horse/hoof cues need completed stables. One ambient event plays every 9–17 seconds, avoiding immediate repetition. Raids suppress ordinary life sounds and trigger quiet hoof/shot cues on their visible phases. Dialogs, settings, hidden tabs and leaving the village stop timers and voices. Music/effects volumes are independent; the audio context is released on exit. Browser autoplay restrictions are retried on a user gesture.

**Economy tuning.** Normal completion pays **one coin per collected gem, plus ten coins per bonus gem remaining on the settled board**. The results screen counts up the saved total with a coin burst and shows both quantities; reduced motion displays the final amount immediately. Prices and payouts are intentionally generous while testing the loop. Chest coins and overflow are additional income shown in their reward receipts. Larger buildings and improvements now take one normal puzzle; basic buildings open immediately. Continuous mode has its separate small allowance so it cannot indefinitely fund the village. Keep decisions to one clear action, without maintenance bills, resource ratios, workers, or real-time construction timers.

**3D presentation.** `TownDiorama.js` uses original Three.js geometry for timber buildings, staged construction, animated articulated residents, horses, and a mine cart. The museum has a porch and crystal displays; armory upgrades add supply crates and storage. A separate continuous rolling prairie has winding tracks, branched trees, hills, sparse grass, rocks, cacti, and fog. The landscape is built once per scene. The overview frames the actual lots for desktop and portrait views; zoom, rotate and reset remain available. The SVG fallback shares the same plot and path layout. Starting construction rebuilds town objects, preserving the camera. Static geometry uses indexed vertex-color batches, while animated parts share instanced geometry and clothing colors. The static view is cached with its depth buffer, so people remain correctly occluded by buildings without redrawing the entire prairie every frame. Camera/building/selection changes and resizing refresh the cache. Pointer camera changes are coalesced per frame; shadows update only when needed. Ambient animation is capped at 30 fps and pixel ratio at 1.5. Windmill rotors and the mine cart share the animated pass. Reduced motion stops ambient animation while manual camera controls remain usable.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, or use the accessible camera buttons. With the canvas focused, arrows rotate, plus/minus zoom, and Home resets the view. The camera remains above terrain. Labels follow the view and hide when they overlap or leave the frame; every plot also remains in the building list. Dragging and pinching do not select plots or enter the mine. Museum dialogs and Settings pause the town. Renderers stay outside Vue’s reactive graph and dispose controls, geometry, materials, textures, observers, and WebGL contexts on exit. The SVG fallback supports the same construction, museum, armory, and mining actions when WebGL is unavailable. The landing illustration reuses original SVG building art and loads no 3D renderer.

**Persistence and accounting.** `localProfile` is the replaceable storage boundary; there is no backend or account requirement. The profile stays under `crystal-cascade-profile-v2` with normal/continuous records, wallet, bounded powers, builder hammers, building stages, projects, events, and run markers. Earlier single-project and campaign-only saves migrate with their recovery key intact. Saved over-cap inventory is converted once into 10 coins per excess item, with a visible explanation; saving/reloading retains the normalized quantities and coin balance. Normal victory saves campaign progress, completed construction, new capacity, pending chest receipts, shop refresh and mining payout together. Claiming a roulette saves its selected award and removes the pending receipt atomically. Continuous receipts validate the active run and track credited earnings independently. Duplicate or stale settlement cannot pay twice.

**Language and tester reset.** All landing, town, museum, armory, puzzle, chest, Settings, sign, and accessibility text follows the first supported browser preference (French or English, otherwise English). A future account setting can use the same locale boundary. Settings → Reset progress for testing asks for confirmation and clears normal/continuous records, allowance usage, powers, hammers, money, buildings, work, raid receipts/counters, and income checkpoints. Cancellation preserves everything. The confirmed reset returns to the bare village and persists across reload.

Existing engine, fusion, input, active-time, reward, camera-clearance, and localization regressions remain in place. The parallel main branch’s expanded fusion behavior and French feedback are preserved.

Progress belongs to this browser; clearing its data can lose the save. Local state is editable and is not an anti-cheat boundary. The separate Symfony work in issue #10 will provide recoverable accounts and authoritative action/reward accounting.
